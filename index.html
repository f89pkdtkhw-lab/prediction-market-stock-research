The hypothesis

If a prediction market reprices an economically important event before a related stock reacts, its probability change may help forecast that stock's subsequent return.

Our test uses a five-minute probability change and a 30-minute stock return adjusted for a sector ETF. A baseline already knows stock, market and sector momentum and time of day.

Event markets→Company rights→Causal timestamps→Forecast / replay

A concrete example

Suppose the chance of a drug approval rises from 70% to 85%. If approval versus rejection would change the company’s value by 10%, that probability move implies roughly a 1.5% value change, all else equal. The research tests whether related stocks react later, after accounting for their own momentum and broader market moves.

Illustrative assumptions, not a measured valuation or a trade recommendation. An 85% probability alone is not a buy signal.

The first finding

No predictive gain

Adding the prediction-market feature made event-weighted forecast MSE about 0.10% higher in this development sample.

The unchanged primary execution threshold generated zero trades on the fixed LLY session. A separately declared small-move diagnostic generated three simulated trades.

Three forecast event families and one quote session cannot establish a general result. Economic mappings are still unapproved.

What is working

Public data adapters, raw-data provenance, minute alignment, event-review queues, daily expanding model fits, a quote replay engine, costs and liquidity limits, and rejection logs. Polymarket is included in data-feasibility work; the broad equity study shown here uses Kalshi.

The initial result is a research decision, not a trading product: qualify the exact event economics and strengthen data collection before using more capital or claiming a business.

Audit trail before a performance claim

  1. Freeze first. Five existing corporate-action cases, one fixed quote session, model features, costs and all seven replay variants were recorded before opening new equity returns.
  2. Keep cases, including zeros. One primary contract per family was chosen by earliest opening and ID tie-break. No substitution for a more active or successful contract.
  3. Use historical clocks. Completed observations only; backward joins; no interpolation. Both PM book endpoints must pass quality checks. New invalid observations block older ones.
  4. Fit on the past. Daily training labels end before the next market open; scalers and coefficients use only that training subset. Minute observations are not treated as independent events.
  5. Separate labels from fills. Stock bars produce forecast labels; full intraminute quotes drive execution simulation. Missing endpoint bars are retained as failed observations.
  6. Quarantine development exposure. All opened families and connected contracts are removed from any future untouched-test claim. One former proposed test component was removed;28 unapproved proposals remain.

793 tests passed. All seven replay variants were reconciled against quotes, sizes, fees and cash. 200 stock/sector labels were independently checked against source bars. The full research pack retains immutable protocols, source hashes, fit clocks and rejection logs.
